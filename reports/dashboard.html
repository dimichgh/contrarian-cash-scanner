Breakout Monitor

Watches the beaten-down cash machines from the Contrarian Cash Scan for the day their price turns and starts the move up. Each name gets a stage, the exact price that would trigger an entry signal, and a readiness score the engine re-learns every week from five years of history.

Watchlist by readiness

Readiness is the engine's estimate that the stock reaches its target before its stop within 40 trading days, shown as a percentile of every setup in its training history: 90 means a top-decile setup. Target and stop are sized to each stock's own volatility: about +10% and −6% for a typical value stock, more for a volatile one. The trigger level is the close that fires an entry signal. Distance is the close against the trigger level: negative means that far below it. The chart shows the last 60 closes with the level dashed, or an arrow at the edge when the level is outside that range.

How the engine improves itself

  1. Every trading day it scores each name from its daily bars, records the call, and raises a signal when a name moves into a new stage.
  2. Every week it re-runs the full fundamental scan: new names that qualify join the watchlist, and names that miss three scans in a row leave it unless their move is under way.
  3. Every week it replays five years of history for about 300 names, labels every day by what happened next, refits the readiness weights, and re-tests every variant of the trigger rule.
  4. Every week it also refits the trigger quality score on past trigger days alone. While that score ranks the final year's triggers better than chance, it decides which triggers count as strong.
  5. Changes need proof. A new rule or a new level of trust in the data replaces the old one only if it does better on the final year, which the fit never sees.
  6. It keeps score. Every call it made is checked against what the price actually did, so the track record above is out of sample.
